Space shows work because the music already exists: Holst, Zimmer, the Voyager story, the aurora. The three shows here each take a different angle on the sky, from a beginner show about the famous photo of Earth to an advanced show about the distance between galaxies and between people.

Pale Blue Dot

Written for Batesburg-Leesville High School in 2025, a 7:05 beginner show named for the Voyager photograph. Hans Zimmer's Day One and Holst's Jupiter open it, Mars follows, Sara Bareilles' Gravity is the ballad, and Jacob Collier's Little Blue brings it home. Written for a small band.

Worlds Apart

Written for Alpharetta High School in 2025, an 8:30 advanced show that uses the distance between stars as a picture of the distance between people. Journey's Separate Ways opens it, Toccata Andromeda drives the second movement, Keane's Somewhere Only We Know is the ballad, and a Hyperjump closer brings Separate Ways back.

True North

Written for Lexington High School in 2025, an 8:00 advanced show about the northern sky: snow, cold air and the aurora. Slalom opens it, Aurora Awakes is the ballad, and Vuelie with Aurora Awakes closes. True North is the one show in the catalog with a full performance video on its page.

What makes a space show land

Contrast. The audience expects big brass and a Holst quote; the shows that win do that and then go quiet. Pale Blue Dot's Gravity and Worlds Apart's Somewhere Only We Know are the moments directors tell us about afterwards. Sound design matters more here than in most themes because a synth pad reads as space instantly.

Buying a space show

Each show is for sale as written, as separate arrangements, or as the start of a partial custom show. A common request is to take the Jupiter opener from Pale Blue Dot and pair it with a different ballad. Licensing goes through Tresona and is paid by the school. Pricing is quoted per program.
