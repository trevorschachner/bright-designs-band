Story shows have a plot: a worker eating lunch on a steel beam, a newsroom on deadline, a revolution, a marionette cutting its strings. Eighteen shows in the catalog carry a narrative the audience can follow from opener to closer, which is also what judges score under general effect.

Shows in this collection

Lunch Atop A Skyscraper, written for Columbine High School in 2025, follows an ironworker through a day above the city on Back to the Future, 9 to 5, Steve Reich's New York Counterpoint and New York, New York. The Times, for Houston County, is a newsroom show on the overture to Newsies, Leroy Anderson's The Typewriter and the Forrest Gump feather theme. Let Them Eat Cake, for Rock Canyon, is a revolution. No Strings Attached, for Alpharetta, is a marionette breaking free. Gold Rush, Off the Rails, Drawn to Life, Art of Rebellion, Excalibur, A Night At The Opera, That's Amore, Gargoyle, Feathered Flame, Head in the Clouds, Spices Perfumes & Toxins, Strings of Fate, Fast Lane and Aromatic are also here.

Why a story helps a band

A story gives every design decision a reason. The ballad is where the character doubts; the closer is where they win. Students learn the show faster because they know what each part means, and directors have a ready answer when a parent asks what the show is about. Judges reward coherence, and a story is the simplest way to be coherent.

Difficulty range

Excalibur and Off the Rails are grade 2. Most of the collection is grade 3 to 4. No Strings Attached, Spices Perfumes & Toxins and Strings of Fate are advanced.

Buying a story show

Each show is for sale as written, as separate arrangements, or as the start of a partial custom show. Story shows adapt well to partial custom: keep the plot and two of the movements, and have us write a new ballad for your soloist. Licensing goes through Tresona and is paid by the school. Pricing is quoted per program.

---faq---
Q: Do story shows need narration or props?
A: No. The stories are told through the music, the drill and the guard. A few original performances used a set piece; none depend on one.

Q: Which story show is easiest?
A: Excalibur, at grade 2, or Off the Rails, also grade 2 and written for a small band.

Q: Can we change the story?
A: Within reason. A new title and a new ballad can shift the plot; a full rewrite is a custom show, and we start those in the winter for music by May 1.
