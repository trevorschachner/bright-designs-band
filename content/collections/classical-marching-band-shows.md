Classical and opera shows give a band music that is out of copyright, plays well in the open air and has already survived two centuries of audiences. The shows here are built mainly on classical and operatic sources, from a beginner Arthurian show on Mozart and Purcell to an advanced marionette show that quotes Pinocchio.

Shows in this collection

A Night At The Opera, written for Westside High School in 2025, pairs Beethoven's Fidelio overture and Rossini's Barber of Seville with Queen: Bohemian Rhapsody, Bicycle Race, Love of My Life and Killer Queen. Excalibur, for Batesburg-Leesville in 2024, is a beginner show on Mozart's Fantasia in D Minor, Purcell's Dido's Lament and John Powell's Test Drive. That's Amore, for Columbine, is an Italian show on Nino Rota's La Strada and Be Italian with Dean Martin's ballad. Let Them Eat Cake, for Rock Canyon, sets a revolution to Boccherini's Minuet, Beethoven's Eroica and I Dreamed a Dream. Aromatic uses Rimsky-Korsakov's Scheherazade and Saint-Saëns' Bacchanale. No Strings Attached is the advanced option.

Why classical sources help a band

Most of the music is public domain, so the licensing bill is lower and faster. The melodies are long and singable, which suits a wind book. And the original scores are rich enough that an arranger can take a theme in a new direction; the Eroica in Let Them Eat Cake becomes a march to the barricades.

Licensing

Public-domain pieces need no licence. Arrangements of recent film and pop pieces inside these shows, like Test Drive or Love of My Life, go through Tresona and are paid by the school. We list which pieces need what before you commit.

Buying a classical show

Each show is for sale as written, as separate arrangements, or as the start of a partial custom show. Building a new show out of public-domain movements from several of these is a common and inexpensive route. Pricing is quoted per program.
