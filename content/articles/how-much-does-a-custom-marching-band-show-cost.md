On every first call there's a moment where the director takes a breath and says something like, "So, budget is always part of the equation." I call it the fun question.

Most designers won't answer it on a website. I think that's annoying when you're trying to put a number in front of a booster board, so here are ours.

| What you're buying | What it costs |
|---|---|
| A custom show from us | Starts at $5,000 |
| A custom show from a high-tier arranger | $8,000 to $15,000 |
| A finished show from our shelf: winds, percussion and sound design | Around $2,500 |

Our custom shows start at $5,000. Most of the high-tier arrangers in this activity charge $8,000 to $15,000. A finished show from our shelf varies with the show, but figure around $2,500 for winds, percussion and sound design.

Those are starting points. Two bands asking for "a custom show" almost never want the same amount of work. One wants a wind book and nothing else. One wants winds, percussion, sound design, drill and somebody to run the whole thing. The rest of this page is what moves your number up or down from there.

## You're buying up to five things

Directors ask us for "a menu," and that's the right way to think about it.

**The wind book.** Brass and woodwind parts for the whole show, written for your instrumentation. This is what Brighton and I do. We're both horn players, and it's the biggest line on most quotes.

**Percussion.** Battery and front ensemble. We aren't percussion writers, so we bring one in, and this line swings more than any other. We know writers at both ends of the range and a few in the middle. A first-year bass line that needs a pulse book shouldn't be paying for a top battery writer.

**Sound design.** Synth patches, samples, the laptop file. I do some of this myself, and for a show that's past my depth we bring in a specialist. The range here is silly. I know people who charge ten times what other people charge for a sound design book. I wouldn't recommend the expensive ones for every band.

**Drill.** Charts for your numbers on your field. It's close to the wind book in size. A director told us he had no idea what the market rate for drill was any more, because he'd paid the same person the same fee for years. That's common. Ask for drill as its own line so you can see it.

**Coordination.** One person who holds the design together across music, drill and guard, and checks in during the season. Ryan does this for us.

## And one thing that isn't on our quote

Licensing. Every copyrighted song in your show needs permission to arrange, and the publisher charges a fee per song. That bill usually goes straight to the school, so it isn't on our quote. It is absolutely in your budget.

Two things to know. The fees have gone up: charts we used to clear for one price a couple of years ago now cost about a third more. And the song choice moves it a lot. A show built on four current pop songs carries four fees. Anything published before 1931 is in the public domain in the US and carries none ([Duke's public domain list](https://cspd.law.duke.edu/publicdomainday/2026/)).

When we're picking music with you, we'll tell you which tunes are going to be expensive before you fall in love with them.

## What moves the number

**How many of the five you need.** If you have a percussion writer who knows your kids, keep them. We'll send them each movement the day you approve it. Plenty of directors do this, and we'd rather work with someone you trust than replace them.

**How much is new.** Four new arrangements cost more than two new ones built around two from our shelf. A finished show bought as written is around $2,500, about half of where custom starts.

**How big and how hard.** More players is more parts. A grade 5 book takes longer to write and to check than a grade 2 book.

**When you call.** December is easy. April means somebody is working nights, and you can guess how that goes. The dates we hold are in [when to start planning](/blog/when-to-start-planning-a-marching-band-show).

## What we'll ask you

We'll ask what your budget is. It isn't a trick. We're flexible, and I'd rather build a package that fits than send a quote that makes you go quiet.

We'll ask when your money shows up. One director's drill money comes out of county stipends that aren't released until November. Fine. We do half up front or a quarter up front depending on what your account looks like, and the rest on delivery or on a date we agree on.

And we'll ask who approves it. If it's your boosters, we'll send a quote with every line broken out and a draft contract you can carry into the meeting.

## What isn't in any design quote

- Flags, costumes and props. The materials and the building.
- Guard choreography, if someone outside your staff writes it.
- Microphones, a mixer, speakers.
- Buses. One director told us he pays about as much per local contest for buses as he does for some lines of his design budget, because the district has no drivers.

Price the props and costumes before you commit to a concept. A show you can afford to have written and can't afford to put on a field is a mistake I've watched happen.

## How to spend less without wrecking the show

- Use one or two finished arrangements and have the rest written.
- Pick public-domain music for a part or two.
- Keep your percussion writer.
- Make the show shorter. Seven and a half minutes is plenty, and fewer minutes is less of everything.
- Build props that don't move.
- Call in December.

And if the whole thing is more than you have, say so. Sometimes the honest answer is that this is a box-show year. We've told bands that, and here's [how we decide](/blog/box-show-or-custom-marching-band-show).

## The part directors don't expect

When a director comes to us after a rough year with another designer, I always ask what went wrong. It's almost never the price. It's music that showed up in July, or three designers who never spoke to each other.

We're not trying to break the bank. Brighton and I both have other jobs. We write shows because we love it, and that's why we start at $5,000 when a lot of people start at $8,000. The thing we're strict about is the calendar.

## How to get a number from us

The numbers at the top get you in the right neighborhood. For yours, send one email with five things: how many winds, your instrumentation, the level you want, which of the five pieces you need, and when your boosters vote. You'll get a range back, and a firm quote after one call.
