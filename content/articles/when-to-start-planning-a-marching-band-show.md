Start in November. I know you just got off the bus from state and the last thing you want is another marching band conversation. Have it anyway, while you still remember what went wrong.

Then two dates run the rest of the year: all your wind music by May 1, and all your drill by Labor Day. Everything else is working backwards from those.

![The seven stages of designing a show, laid against the school year from November to Labor Day](../how-to-design-a-marching-band-show/fig-calendar.svg "The design year. Two dates hold it up: music by May 1 and drill by Labor Day.")

## Why May 1

I don't do well with things happening over the summer. I try to get as much done as I can before school is out, and after ten years of writing shows I've stopped apologising for it.

May 1 is first drafts of everything. The whole wind book, in your hands, before spring camp. If your school runs later and spring camp is in June, we'll move it. The date matters less than the reason for it, which is that your kids get to play the show before they leave for the summer.

That one read-through tells you more than any score study. You hear whether the second trumpets can hold that, whether the ballad sits, whether the closer is too much. Then we fix it in May, when fixing it is easy.

Two other people are waiting on May 1 too. Your percussion writer needs the finished wind score to write to, and April and May, right after indoor season, is when percussion writers have time. And your drill writer can't write a single set until the music is final, because every set is tied to a count.

## Why Labor Day

By Labor Day the band should be done learning and starting to clean. Drill that shows up in mid-September gets taught during the same weeks you wanted for cleaning, with contests already underway.

Drill comes a movement at a time. The opener lands around July 15 so you can teach it at camp, then another movement every two or three weeks. Tell your drill writer your camp dates and how many sets a day your band can learn, and the schedule gets built around that.

## The thing that holds drill up is you

I say this with love. Some of our bands, and my South Carolina groups are notorious for it, don't have numbers until band camp starts.

We can't turn out three movements of drill in an evening. If we get your numbers the first day of camp, you're getting drill late, and we were ready in June.

So: a real number by July 1. It can change. Re-check it with your drill writer before each movement. But give us something to write to.

If you honestly don't know, tell us, and we'll write drill for the kids you're sure of. The maybes come on for the big moments and stand behind a prop the rest of the time. That works better than it sounds.

## The year, month by month

**November.** Sit down with your staff the week after your last show and go caption by caption. What worked, what you'd change, which sections carried it. Then decide who's designing next year. December and January are quiet months for arrangers, so a show agreed before winter break gets started within a couple of weeks.

**December.** Get a number to your boosters. Most boards set the budget in January or February and can't vote on "we're talking to some people." Ask for a quote with each piece of work on its own line and a draft contract you can carry into the meeting.

**December and January.** Concept. One or two calls. You bring your roster and whatever ideas you've got, we bring a few of ours, and we build a cut track: the recordings spliced together at show length so everybody can hear the pacing before a note is written.

**February.** Eighth-grade night. You won't know your roster until summer, but this is when you learn the most about it. Licensing requests go in the same week the music is picked. A lot of songs clear in a day. Some take weeks, and a few come back as a no.

**Early March.** Every piece is chosen.

**March and April.** We write. You'll see drafts a movement at a time, and each one goes to your percussion writer the day you approve it.

**May 1.** The book is in your hands. Read it at spring camp and send us one list of edits.

**June.** Lock your props and equipment. Whoever writes the drill needs to know what's on the field.

**July 1.** Numbers.

**Mid-July to Labor Day.** Drill, a movement at a time.

## What it looks like when it slips

A director told us about the years before he called. He'd get to August and ask where part three was. Whatever first draft showed up, he had to take, because there wasn't time to change it. Then the visual staff built moments around music they'd had no say in.

We were having that conversation in late February. He told us the best part of the call was the date on it.

Other versions we've heard this year: drill that arrived at 4 a.m. on the day it was to be taught. A percussion book that came six weeks after the winds and pushed everything behind it. Sound files that showed up the week of the home show.

Every one of those started with a date somebody let go by in the spring.

## If you're already late

**It's January or February.** You're fine. Sign with somebody this month.

**It's March or April.** We can't write you a custom book by May 1, and nobody else can do it well either. We do push a few schools to June 1 every year because they called too late, and I'd rather not. Buy a finished show and have it re-scored for your band.

**It's May or later.** Buy a finished show as written, today, and call a drill writer tomorrow.

**You just took the job.** The music may already be locked. Get everyone who's writing for you on one call in your first two weeks and ask what's still open.

---faq---
Q: Is November too early to plan next year's show?
A: No. It's the best month for it. You still remember the season, and arrangers start writing in December and January.

Q: When should the music be finished?
A: May 1, or before your spring camp if that's later. June 1 is the outside edge.

Q: When should drill be finished?
A: Labor Day, delivered a movement at a time starting around July 15.

Q: How long does music licensing take?
A: Many songs clear in a day and some take weeks. Bands of America's handbook says to ask at least three months before arranging starts. Send requests the week you choose the music.

Q: What if we don't know our numbers?
A: Give your drill writer the number you're sure of by July 1. Drill gets written for those students and the rest are added for the big moments.

Q: Can we still get a show in April?
A: Yes, a finished one. Custom music started in April won't be ready when you need it.
